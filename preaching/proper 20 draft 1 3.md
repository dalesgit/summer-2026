## Settings of old narratives
[[proper 20 draft outline]]

The readings from the Bible that we’ve heard this morning, each of them in Their Own Way, Reflect how the world around them had gone awry.

Jonah, such a strange figure, and a strange little book bundled with all the other major and minor profits. We’re familiar with the major outlines: Jonah being contacted by God with a mission. Jonah saying in response is there somebody else up there? And trying to run away. Only to be rescued by God and seemingly reluctantly heading off to his assignment, preaching repentance to the Ninevites. They repented and God relented, much to Jonah’s irritation. That’s where we pick it up in today’s reading.
God looks down, sees terrible mischief going on. Assigned someone to warn the offenders, and they are reluctant to follow the voice of God.

Jesus tells a parable to his followers many centuries later. He wants them to become prophetic witnesses to God‘s Kingdom. They are reluctant. Well maybe that’s an ongoing theme?

So in a common pattern, Jesus tells them a story, a parable. It’s a parable is about workers and how the manager seems to show no partiality. How might we tell that in today’s world? In a world where workers are generally exploited. This part of the country where the historical representatives of workers, the unions, are virtually outlawed.
How do we tell a parable in today’s setting where our expectations of what is appropriate and fair are overthrown by God‘s overwhelming compensation?

If God were to speak to one in our midst, how would we recognize it? Who could Command enough respect from the folks of Palm Harbor in Florida that we would drop everything and try to fulfill our assignment?
