## Opening hook

[[proper 20 draft 1]]

It’s amazing to me to think that just a month ago I was standing behind a church in Seydisfjordur, Iceland. A little tiny village of a little over 600 people on the north-eastern side of the island nation. Until fairly recently it was a major town on the island being a good distribution point for the fishing which was the main business of the population.

I was looking down on an archaeological dig from a few years ago that had uncovered a house built around the time of the earliest Viking settlement ca. 1,000 years ago. 

I felt a sense of connection to folks long, long ago. At that point the Vikings were not yet Christianized. That would come in a couple of centuries. I felt somehow related in ways I can’t explain.

A few days later we woke up on the ship hoping to be able to see a total eclipse of the sun. For many there was a sense of disappointment as we were mostly covered with clouds during the actual event. But not me. I caught some glimpses of the moon as it moved in between us and the sun -- a shadow moving across the sun, if you will. I felt a sense of connection to the universe itself. Stars, planets, -- so much bigger than me. Consider that just a few hundred years ago it was a hotly contested notion whether we lived in a solar system with the sun in the center of things.

> Come to think of it, there are still some who would dispute that kind of basic reality. Those who would claim to be speaking for Christianity while reading the book of Genesis literally. Such folks miss out on so much. They miss the profound sense of connection that I felt then -- a kind of echo of my sense that we are all of us just made of bits of stardust.

We live in a strange reality these days.

## It’s all energy and relationships

My travels gave me a sense of **connection** to the universe. You’ve heard from me and others that “**we are stardust**”. That’s what that means. 

## things are amiss

In ancient times a total solar eclipse was a sign from the universe (the gods?) that a mighty national empire might be overthrown. It could be a strong enough sign that whole populations might move from one place to another. 

Today it’s a photo-op. Or a missed photo-op as the case may be.

When I look around I see signs that things are amiss. For me it was more obvious as we traveled around the north Atlantic, following the path that Norse sailors had sailed a thousand years ago. 

If we have eyes to see and ears to hear ... But it appears more and more that we don’t have eyes and ears, because we have covered them up.
